# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

AgentMux contributors, maintainers, and community members commit to providing a
welcoming, safe, and constructive environment regardless of experience,
identity, background, ability, appearance, or personal characteristics.

## Expected behavior

- Discuss ideas and code with empathy, patience, and technical honesty.
- Give actionable feedback and distinguish facts from preferences.
- Respect boundaries, privacy, attribution, and confidentiality.
- Accept responsibility, apologize when appropriate, and repair harm.
- Focus on what is best for the project and its users.
- Handle security reports and sensitive diagnostics through private channels.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, discrimination, stalking, or unwanted sexual attention.
- Personal attacks, insults, deliberate intimidation, or sustained disruption.
- Publishing private information, credentials, or communications without
  permission.
- Impersonation, deceptive attribution, or knowingly misleading security claims.
- Retaliation against someone who raises a good-faith concern.
- Any conduct that would be inappropriate in a professional community.

## Scope

This policy applies in project repositories, issues, reviews, discussions,
events, and other spaces where someone represents AgentMux. It also applies to
private interactions that materially affect community safety.

## Reporting and enforcement

For sensitive conduct reports, use the repository's private **Security → Report
a vulnerability** channel and mark the report as a code-of-conduct matter. If
that channel is unavailable, contact a maintainer privately without posting
personal or sensitive details publicly.

Maintainers receiving reports must protect confidentiality as far as practical,
avoid conflicts of interest, gather necessary context, and respond
proportionately. Possible actions include clarification, a private warning,
content removal, temporary participation restrictions, or a permanent ban.
Immediate protective action may be taken when credible safety risks exist.

Anyone directly involved in a report must recuse themselves from deciding its
outcome. Retaliation and knowingly false reports may themselves result in
enforcement. Good-faith reports are protected even when an investigation does
not confirm a violation.

## Appeals

A person subject to enforcement may request one private review by an
uninvolved maintainer. The appeal should identify a factual or procedural error;
disagreement alone does not require reversal. The review decision is final for
the incident.
